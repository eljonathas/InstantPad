# 0007: The user trains a voice inside the app

[ADR 0006](0006-trained-voice-models.md) made a voice speak through an RVC model trained on it, and left the training to a kit run from a terminal, on Linux or WSL, with Python and PyTorch installed by hand. That suits the developer, who trains the voices the app ships. The users who clone a voice cannot do it: they have no terminal, no WSL and no Python. They have to train their own voices from the app, with nothing installed beforehand. Distributing the voices trained by the developer is a separate decision.

- **The app downloads the training tools when the user asks to train, and only then.** They are uv, which installs a Python 3.12 build it checks against its own hashes; PyTorch and the rest of what RVC imports; RVC's scripts at the pinned commit, as a zip instead of a git clone; and RVC's base models. Every file is pinned by its size and SHA-256, as the models of ADR 0003 are. `tools/voice-training/pin.py` resolves them once for each computer into `environments/`, so the app downloads each wheel itself, shows the progress and resumes a download that stopped. With an NVIDIA card that comes to about 4.2 GB, 3.3 GB of it PyTorch with CUDA, and about 9 GB on disk. On the processor it comes to about 1.2 GB, and 3 GB on disk. The user sees the size before anything downloads and agrees to it.
- **The training is the kit's, run by the app.** The app runs `voicekit.py train` and `export` as a child process, on Windows itself, with no WSL, from the copy of the kit built into the app. With `--events`, the kit writes one JSON line for each step and each epoch, which the app reads for the progress. The ONNX export, and the check against PyTorch, are the ones the terminal runs, and the result is added to the voice through `TrainedVoiceModels::add`, as a folder picked by hand is. The kit stays the one way to train, for the developer and for the app. It changes where it has to: RVC comes as a zip, FFmpeg comes from a wheel, and the dataset can be written into the kit's folder directly.
- **Only an NVIDIA card trains on the card.** PyTorch on Windows only runs well on CUDA: DirectML is stuck on old versions of PyTorch, ROCm for Windows is a preview, and Intel's XPU builds are new. The app asks `nvidia-smi` for the card and its memory. A card of 4 GB or more trains there, and the batch grows with the memory. AMD, Intel, an older driver or a smaller card train on the processor. That works, but takes more than a day, and the app says so, with its estimate, before the user agrees to anything.
- **Windows and Linux, not macOS.** RVC's training on Apple's GPU is unreliable, and Intel Macs only have the processor. On macOS the voice keeps only "Add a trained model...". Linux runs the same code as Windows.
- **The user records or chooses 10 to 40 minutes of speech.** That is what RVC needs to learn a voice well, and more adds little. The minutes come in takes of up to 10 minutes each, recorded at the microphone or decoded from files the user picks, which are written to the training's folder as they come. A whole recording never has to fit in memory. A voice is trained from a cloned voice and attaches its model to it. The feminine voice is not trained in the app: it will ship trained.
- **The recordings stay on disk only while the training needs them.** [ADR 0005](0005-voice-cloning-without-checks.md) keeps a recording in memory only. Training reads hours of data from disk, so this ADR amends it for training alone: the takes, and the pieces RVC cuts and analyzes from them, live in the training's own folder. They are deleted as soon as the model is in the voice or the user discards the training. Nothing else of the voice is kept beyond the model.
- **Consent is cloning's, as ADR 0005 has it.** There are no terms and no digits to read. The training dialog says, as the README does, that the app does not check whose voice it is, and that passing oneself off as someone is the user's to answer for.
- **The disk is checked before anything is written.** Everything lives in the app's data folder. The app refuses to start when the free space is short of the download, the install and a training together, and says how much is missing. The tools stay after a training, so the next voice trains with no download, and the voice screen offers to remove them, showing how much room that frees.
- **A training can stop and resume.** RVC saves where it is every few epochs: every 5 on a card, every one on the processor. Stopping ends the kit and every process it started; on Windows they all run in a job object that ends with the app. Resuming runs the kit again, and RVC goes on from its last save. One training runs at a time. It goes on with the dialog closed, with the progress on the voice screen. Closing the app pauses it. When the app opens again, the voice screen shows the epoch it reached, and the user resumes it from there.
- **Tests need no card, network or microphone.** The training is a port, `VoiceTrainer`. The use case is tested against a fake, the adapter against the contract tests of its port with a stand-in for the kit, and the reading of the kit's events and of `nvidia-smi` on their own.

The rules live in `crates/domain/src/training.rs`, the use case in `crates/application/src/training.rs`, the adapter in `crates/voice-ai/src/training`, and the kit in `tools/voice-training`.
