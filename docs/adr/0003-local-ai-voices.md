# 0003: AI voices run locally, on models downloaded on demand

The AI voices change the timbre of the user's voice, not only its pitch. They had to keep three promises of the app: a free download that stays small, no account or server, and nothing the user says leaving the computer.

So the models run on the user's processor with ONNX Runtime, and nothing is uploaded. The installer does not grow: the runtime library and the models, about 180 MB, download the first time an AI voice is used, from pinned URLs, and each file is checked against a size and a SHA-256 kept in the code.

- **Conversion** uses the tone color converter of OpenVoice V2 (MIT), from an ONNX export pinned to one revision. A measurement on a 4-core 2.1 GHz Xeon converted 139 ms of voice in 95 to 120 ms, in chunks with 197 ms of past audio and 35 ms ahead. Smaller chunks lose quality and larger ones add delay. Half of that time goes to the past audio, which is converted again at every chunk and thrown away, so shorter chunks cost more than their share and leave less room on a slow processor.
- **The user's own voice** must be known to take it out of the voice. A voiceprint averaged over a few seconds of reading was clearly better than one estimated from each chunk, so the AI learns it once. It does so by itself, with nothing for the user to record: when an AI voice is picked and the voice is not known yet, the microphone is listened to while the user talks, and eight seconds of talk are enough, counting only the parts where someone talks (not silence, a steady noise or a click). Then the voice comes on. A fixed average voice in its place would work with no listening at all, but it sounds worse for voices that are far from the average. Learning again is one key, for a noisy first time or another microphone.
- **Models** are the tone color converter and its voiceprint extractor, and nothing else. A speech recognizer used to download with them, to read back the digits of the cloning check that [ADR 0005](0005-voice-cloning-without-checks.md) removed, and went with it.
- **Feminine AI** speaks with the average voiceprint of ten LibriSpeech speakers, so it imitates no real person. The converter keeps the melody of the voice, so the effect raises the pitch first.
- **ONNX Runtime 1.23.2** is the last version with an Intel macOS build, and the app ships one. The `ort` crate loads the library at run time and asks it only for an old API version. The library comes from the official PyPI wheel, which is a zip on every system.
- **The audio callback never waits for the AI.** It hands the voice to a worker thread through a ring and plays what comes back once a chunk is buffered. The output starts with the first chunk, after 100 ms of silence that absorbs a chunk that comes a little late. Waiting for more than one chunk before starting, as it did at first, cost a whole chunk of delay at the start and again after every late chunk, which only grew: measured on that Xeon, the first sound came after 440 ms and the delay settled between 430 and 790 ms, against 370 ms and 375 to 460 ms after the change. What is left is the time the model takes, which only a faster processor or a smaller window reduces. When the worker falls behind, the app warns and keeps only the pitch part of the effect, so a slow computer never stutters in a match.

GPU acceleration is left out: the game already uses the GPU, and a voice that lowers the frame rate is worse than one that arrives a little later.
